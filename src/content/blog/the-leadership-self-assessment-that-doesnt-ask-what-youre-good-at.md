Most leadership self-assessments ask you some version of the same question: *how good are you at this?*

It sounds reasonable. It isn't. Ask a new lead how good they are at delegation and you'll get one of two answers: a modest 6 out of politeness, or a confident 8 because nobody has told them otherwise. Neither number tells you what to do next.

So I built one that asks something else.

## "I am happy with..."

Every statement in the template starts the same way: **I am happy with...**

- ...the constructive feedback I give to others
- ...my approach to 1:1s
- ...how safe my team feels to speak up, disagree and admit mistakes
- ...the confidence my team and I have using flow metrics to improve delivery

You're not grading your competence. You're rating your **satisfaction**. That small shift changes the conversation you have with yourself. "Am I good at hiring?" invites defensiveness. "Am I happy with how I hire?" invites honesty. You can be decent at something and still not be happy with it, and that tension is exactly where growth starts.

## Private by default

The second design choice: **you don't have to share it with anyone**. Not your manager, not your team, not me.

Self-assessments that end up in a performance review stop being self-assessments. They become negotiations. People round up, hide the uncomfortable scores, and learn nothing.

My suggestion is simple: fill it in alone. Then, if you want support on something, *you* decide to bring it to your manager. The tool starts the conversation; it doesn't replace it.

## Two questions, one gap

Satisfaction alone isn't enough. A 4 on "Presentation" might be a burning problem or completely irrelevant, depending on your role right now.

So version 1.2 asks two questions for each of the 29 skills:

- **Satisfaction**: how happy are you with it today? (1 to 10)
- **Relevance**: how much does it matter for you and your team *right now*? (1 to 10)

The spreadsheet then calculates the **Gap**: Relevance minus Satisfaction. A high gap means "this matters now, and I'm not happy with it." That's where your energy should go.

A note on the word choice. The first draft of this column was called *Importance*. I changed it because importance sounds timeless, and almost everything in leadership feels important. You'd end up with 8s everywhere. *Relevance* is anchored in the present: you can honestly give Hiring a 3 if you aren't hiring this quarter, without feeling like you're dismissing it forever.

## Five priorities, not twenty-nine

Nobody improves 29 things at once. The Output Graphs tab shows your **Top 5** skills, sorted by gap, so the result of the exercise is a short list instead of a wall of numbers.

Next to each skill there's a **Next Step** column. Write one small thing you'll try. Not a plan, not an OKR. One thing.

Then fill it in again in a few months and compare.

## What it covers

The skills are grouped into four areas:

- **People**: feedback, 1:1s, coaching, delegation, hiring, onboarding, conflict
- **Team Health**: health checks, psychological safety, retrospectives, celebrating successes
- **Delivery**: mission, strategy, flow metrics, ways of working, tech debt, saying no
- **Self**: learning, communication, assertiveness, managing up, your own energy

That last one is not a filler. A lead running on empty makes every other score worse.
